Model architecture

Layered, transparent scoring

Each layer turns the text into something more abstract. Raw words become features such as hype terms, attribution phrases, ALL-CAPS ratio and feature mentions. Detectors convert those features into risk signals from 0 to 100.

The signals are averaged with weights and blended with the two strongest, so one severe red flag is never hidden. In parallel, two machine-learning classifiers read the same text: Naive Bayes and Logistic Regression trained on labelled examples of word, bigram and style features.

A meta-learner, fitted with cross-validation, learns how far to trust each model and outputs the final fake likelihood. Rules catch obvious manipulation; the models catch subtle fakes that use no trigger words. Hover the stack to separate the layers, or open the ML Lab to see each model's accuracy.

// 1. rule engine
weighted = Σ(riskᵢ × weightᵢ) / Σ weightᵢ
rule %   = σ((0.75 × weighted + 0.25 × top-2 − c) / 10)
// 2. text classifiers on hashed word + bigram features x
z_nb     = log P(fake | x) − log P(real | x)
z_lr     = w · x + b
// 3. stacked meta-learner (weights learned out-of-fold)
fake %   = σ(a₁·z_nb + a₂·z_lr + a₃·logit(rule %) + a₀)
// sensitivity shifts the final logit: strict +0.5 · lenient −0.5
Signals

What each detector looks for

Hover or tap a card to flip it and see why the signal matters.

Fake News Detector

Sensational language

Clickbait and conspiracy phrases such as "shocking", "exposed" or "they don't want you to know".

Flip for why

Why it matters

Fabricated stories are written to go viral. Hype grabs attention while credible newsrooms follow neutral style guides.

Emotional manipulation

Fear, anger and outrage words: "terrifying", "traitors", "disgusting".

Flip for why

Why it matters

Strong emotions reduce critical thinking and increase sharing, which is exactly what misinformation relies on.

Source attribution

Named officials, studies, agencies, quotes and data versus vague "sources say".

Flip for why

Why it matters

Real reporting lets you trace every claim back to someone accountable. Anonymous, unverifiable sourcing is a red flag.

Writing style

ALL-CAPS shouting, repeated "!!!", emoji and very short fragmented sentences.

Flip for why

Why it matters

Professional editors remove shouting and excessive punctuation. These patterns are typical of chain messages.

Extraordinary claims

Miracle cures, "100% guaranteed", and pressure to "share before it's deleted".

Flip for why

Why it matters

Extraordinary claims need extraordinary evidence. Health hoaxes are among the most frequently debunked topics.

Domain credibility

Trusted outlets, official .gov/.edu, satire sites, look-alike domains and URL shorteners.

Flip for why

Why it matters

Fake sites often imitate real brands (e.g. "news.com.co") or use cheap domain endings to look legitimate.

Fake Review Detector

Extreme sentiment

Stacks of superlatives ("amazing", "perfect", "must buy") or pure rage, with no nuance.

Flip for why

Why it matters

Paid reviews over-praise to lift ratings, while attack reviews from competitors over-criticise.

Product detail

Mentions of real features (battery, fit, delivery), usage time and context.

Flip for why

Why it matters

Fake reviewers have never used the product, so their text is generic and could describe anything.

Repetition

Repeated words and phrases, and product names stuffed in several times.

Flip for why

Why it matters

Template and bot-generated reviews reuse phrasing, and keyword stuffing is an SEO trick in paid reviews.

Promotion & incentives

Links, coupon codes, "visit our store" or "received free in exchange for a review".

Flip for why

Why it matters

Honest reviews don't advertise. Incentivised reviews are measurably more positive than organic ones.

Linguistic pattern

Heavy first-person focus ("I, me, my"), ALL-CAPS and emoji spam.

Flip for why

Why it matters

Research on deceptive opinion spam (Ott et al., 2011) found fabricated reviews use more first-person words and less concrete detail.

Rating consistency

Does a 5-star rating come with negative text, or a 1-star with praise? Is it a verified purchase?

Flip for why

Why it matters

Mismatches reveal copy-pasted or manipulated reviews, and unverified buyers are more likely to be fake.

Reading the score

The fake likelihood is the probability the content is fake. The confidence shows how sure the model is, and it drops for very short inputs or scores close to 50%.

0 – 39%Likely Real / Genuine

Neutral language, sources and specific detail. No major red flags.

40 – 64%Suspicious, verify it

Mixed signals. Cross-check with trusted sources before trusting or sharing.

65 – 100%Likely Fake

Strong manipulation patterns. Do not share without independent confirmation.

FAQ

Questions & limitations

Is the AI always right?

No automated detector is perfect. TruthLens AI looks for patterns that commonly appear in fake content, so well-written misinformation can score low and unusual genuine writing can score high. Use it as a first filter, not a final judgement.

Does it check facts against the internet?

The current version analyses how content is written and where it comes from, not whether each fact is true. That is why the Verified News page links to professional fact-checkers for claim-level verification.

Is my data sent anywhere?

When you open the site directly, everything runs in your browser (in a background Web Worker) and nothing leaves your device. When it is served by the TruthLens Node.js backend, text is sent to that server for analysis; it is only stored there if you give feedback. You can force browser-only analysis in Settings. History always stays in your browser's local storage.

What does the sensitivity setting do?

It shifts the centre of the scoring curve. "Strict" flags content earlier (fewer missed fakes, more false alarms) and "Lenient" does the opposite. "Balanced" is the default.

Does it use machine learning?

Yes. A Naive Bayes and a Logistic Regression classifier are trained on labelled examples and stacked with the rule engine by a meta-learner. Accuracy, F1 and AUC are measured with k-fold cross-validation and shown in the ML Lab. The built-in training set is small, so for real-world accuracy train the models on a public dataset such as LIAR, WELFake or a labelled opinion-spam corpus.

Does it learn from me?

Yes. On every result you can say whether the verdict was right. The model updates immediately (online learning) and keeps the correction for future checks. You can also upload a whole labelled CSV or JSON dataset in the ML Lab and retrain from scratch.

How fast is it?

A typical check takes about a millisecond of compute, because the models are compact linear models over hashed features and repeated inputs are cached. The ML Lab has a benchmark that measures the throughput on your machine.
